Scope: US only. Every figure is dated. Anything older than about one month (before roughly 2026-09-07) is marked **[STALE]**. One-paragraph regime summary: As of early Oct 2026 the US is in a **re-tightening, stagflation-tinged regime with calm equity volatility**. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's FOMC raised rates to 3.75–4.00% on 2026-09-16, the first hike since 2023. The dots point to one more hike in 2026, and markets have trimmed that pricing after a weak September jobs report. Brent oil sits near $100 because the Iran war (which began 2026-02-28) keeps disrupting the Strait of Hormuz. The 10y Treasury is about 5.3%, reported as the highest since 2002, and the 2s10s curve is positive (about +45–52bp). Despite this, the S&P 500 closed at a record 7,818.93 on 2026-10-06 (+14.2% YTD). Spot VIX is about 15, the VIX futures curve is in contango (VIX/VIX3M about 0.85), and SPX implied vol is above realized (about 13% vs about 10.4%). Bond vol (MOVE) is rising while the VIX keeps making lower highs. The main event cluster runs Oct 13 to Nov 3: bank earnings, Sept CPI on Oct 14, VIX expiry on Oct 21, the FOMC on Oct 27–28, Q3 GDP and Sept PCE on Oct 29, and the midterms on Nov 3.

## Fed policy: target range, 2026 decisions, dot plot / market path, balance sheet, leadership

### Takeaway
The Fed is **hiking again**. It raised rates 25bp to 3.75–4.00% on 2026-09-16 (unanimous), and the SEP median (4.1% for end-2026) implies one more 25bp hike, most likely in December. The market had moved toward a hold for October after the weak Oct 2 jobs report. Kevin Warsh is chair, has cut back forward guidance, and has said press conferences may not follow every meeting. That adds event-uncertainty around FOMC days.

### Inferences
- Policy is now **two-sided and data-dependent**: hawkish dots, a soft labor print, and oil-driven headline inflation all pull in different directions. Each CPI, jobs and FOMC event can therefore move rates, and through rates move equity multiples. That favors defined-risk structures through event dates over naked short premium.
- The July hawkish dissents and the hike-leaning dots make December the live meeting. October (Oct 27–28) is priced mostly as a hold, so a surprise hike there would be a vol event.
- Less forward guidance under Warsh, plus the chance of meetings with no press conference, could make FOMC-day moves larger and less predictable than in the Powell era. This is an inference; I found no data on realized FOMC-day moves under Warsh.

## Macro data: inflation, labor, GDP, recession odds, Treasury yields and curve

### Takeaway
Growth is decent: Q2 GDP was revised up to 2.2%. Headline inflation is elevated from the oil shock: CPI is 3.4% y/y and PCE 3.4% y/y. Core measures are mixed: core CPI 2.4% vs core PCE 3.0%, and that gap is unusual. The labor market is clearly softening: +29k payrolls in September, unemployment at 4.2%, wage growth 3.0%, the lowest since 2021. Long yields are at multi-decade highs (10y about 5.3%) with a positive 2s10s of about +45–52bp. The curve is **bear-steepening** in a stagflation-tinged setup.

### Inferences
- The macro mix is stagflation-lite: real growth around 2%, headline inflation around 3.4% driven by energy, a cooling labor market, and the Fed hiking into it. That raises the odds of **rate-driven equity drawdowns**, such as a 10y break above 5.5%, more than growth-scare drawdowns in the near term. A string of weak payrolls, however, could flip the narrative quickly toward "Fed overtightening."
- Rising rate vol (MOVE) against a sleepy VIX is a classic early-warning divergence. Equity vol may be underpricing the macro risk.
- The core PCE vs core CPI gap and the annual PCE revisions make inflation releases noisier to interpret, which raises surprise risk on Oct 14 (CPI) and Oct 29 (PCE).

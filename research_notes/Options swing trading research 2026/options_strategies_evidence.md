## 1. Volatility risk premium (VRP) harvesting: short puts, put credit spreads, iron condors, strangles on SPX/SPY/ETFs

### Takeaway
A VRP exists and has been documented for decades (index puts are overpriced on average). Benchmark short-vol indexes (PUT, BXM, CNDR) deliver equity-like or lower returns with less drawdown than the S&P 500, but they have lagged badly in strong bull years (2020, 2023-2025). Recent academic work also argues the traded VRP has declined. Leveraged or naked short-vol structures, short VIX ETPs, and small accounts on margin are the ones that blow up (Feb 2018, Aug 2024, April 2025). Unlevered, cash-secured index put-writing does not. Since 2007, PUT's Sharpe (0.49) has been *below* the S&P 500's (0.6). Defined-risk versions (put credit spreads, CNDR iron condor, BFLY iron butterfly) cut volatility and tail months further but pay away part of the VRP through the overpriced long wing. No rigorous post-2020 matched comparison of spreads vs naked was found.

### Cited Findings
**Academic evidence for the premium**
- (WP/PR citations within an arXiv preprint) The profitability of systematic short S&P 500 puts is attributed to the "overpriced puts puzzle" and the VRP. Bondarenko (2014) documents that historical SPX puts were significantly overpriced and produced large negative returns for buyers, a pattern standard equilibrium models fail to reproduce. Coval & Shumway ("Expected Option Returns", 2001) find put options have expected returns below the risk-free rate (S&P 500/100 index options). Israelov & Klein (2016) are cited for an asymmetric premium in SPX puts. — [Harvesting the VRP: A Learning-to-Rank Approach, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.24786)
- (WP, SSRN 5525882, Sept 2025) Dew-Becker & Giglio, "The Decline of the Variance Risk Premium: Evidence from Traded and Synthetic Options". Synthetic options built from the underlying over roughly 100 years never had negative alpha, which suggests equity investors never required high compensation for market downturns. An intermediary-based model explains both synthetic and traded option patterns, including the *recent decline in the VRP*. This bears directly on forward expectations for short-vol returns. — [SSRN 5525882](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/5525882.pdf?abstractid=5525882&mirid=1)
- (WP/PR, AFA/AEA 2024) Heston & Todorov, "Exploring the Variance Risk Premium Across Assets" (20 futures markets; keywords include COVID). Option-implied variance does not positively predict underlying futures returns for most assets, but it *does* predict returns to variance-sensitive option portfolios. In other words, IV level is a timing variable for short-vol P&L, not for direction. — [AFA program](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=41580); [AEA 2024](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2024/program/paper/hiTeT8SE)
- (PR, Carr & Wu, RFS 2009) "Variance Risk Premia" is the foundational measurement of negative variance risk premia in index options. — [Carr & Wu, RFS 2009 PDF](https://engineering.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/2019-01/CarrReviewofFinStudiesMarch2009-a.pdf)
- (PRAC) Practitioner summary: "The Variance Risk Premium is Pervasive". — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/the-variance-risk-premium-is-pervasive/)
- (PRAC, tastytrade 2020 slide deck) SPY 45-DTE strangles managed at 21 DTE. For a 16-delta strangle, 32% expected vs 25% actual probability of finishing ITM. For a 30-delta strangle, 60% expected vs 57% actual. The deck concludes that premium overstates realized moves. — [tastytrade Sosnoff India 2020 deck](https://s3.amazonaws.com/tastytradepublicmedia/website/cms/tastytrade_TomSosnoff_India2020.pdf/original/tastytrade_TomSosnoff_India2020.pdf)

**Cboe benchmark indexes (EX; note that pre-launch history is backfilled)**
- PUT index construction: sells one-month ATM SPX puts on the third Friday, holds to expiration, rolls, and is fully collateralized in T-bills (unlevered). — [arXiv 2608.24786](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.24786)
- PUT factsheet, Jan 2007 to Sep 30, 2025: PUT annualized return 6.7%, volatility 10.9%, max DD -32.7%, Sharpe 0.49. S&P 500 TR over the same window: 10.7%, 15.5%, -50.9%, 0.6. So in the post-2007 sample, *unlevered ATM put-writing had a lower Sharpe than simply owning the index*. — [Cboe PUT factsheet (via search snippet)](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/indices/factsheet/CboeGlobalIndices_PUT-Index.pdf)
- Bondarenko (2019, Cboe-commissioned) found PUT had higher risk-adjusted returns than the other five indexes studied (Sharpe, Sortino, Stutzer), but with strongly negative skewness: PUT -2.09 vs S&P 500 -0.81. — [Bondarenko 2019 PDF](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/education/research_publications/PutWriteCBOE19_v14_by_Prof_Oleg_Bondarenko_as_of_June_14.pdf); [Cboe insight](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/white-paper-shows-volatility-risk-premium-facilitated-higher-risk-adjusted-returns-for-put-index/)
- PUT calendar-year returns: +2.1% in 2020 vs S&P 500 TR +18.4%, and -7.7% in 2022 vs S&P 500 TR -18.1%. Cboe says PUT had lower long-term volatility and lower max drawdown than the S&P 500. — [Cboe PUT factsheet (via search snippet; direct fetch blocked)](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/indices/factsheet/CboeGlobalIndices_PUT-Index.pdf)
- (PRAC/asset-manager white paper hosted by Cboe) Neuberger Berman, June 1986 to Dec 2022: PutWrite max drawdown -32.7% vs S&P 500 -50.9%. The PutWrite indexes "declined less and had more expedient recoveries" in almost all cases. — [Neuberger Berman "Simply Put(Writing)"](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/indices/whitepapers/Neuberger_Berman_Simply_PutWriting.pdf)
- (Bondarenko 2016 study, summarized by CXO; data 1986-2015) Max drawdowns: PUT -33%, WPUT (weekly) -24%, S&P 500 -51%. PUT outperformed BXM by about 1.1%/yr, which is attributed to a construction quirk (PUT misses about 4 hours/month of S&P return relative to BXM). Pre-2007 PUT and pre-2015 WPUT histories are retroactively constructed. — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-options/performance-of-cboe-putwrite-indexes/)
- (PRAC, AQR) PutWrite and BuyWrite are economically near-equivalent by put-call parity, so differences come from construction details. — [AQR PutWrite vs BuyWrite](https://images.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/White-Papers/AQR-PutWrite-vs-BuyWritevF.pdf)
- CNDR design: sells about 0.20-delta monthly SPX puts and calls, buys about 0.05-delta wings, and holds T-bills. 2025 price-level change was about flat (+0.10% Barchart, +0.17% FXEmpire snapshots; these are third-party quotes, not official TR). — [Cboe BFLY/CNDR insight](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/benchmark-indices-series-volatility-management-with-cboes-bfly-and-cndr-indices/); [Barchart CNDR](https://www.barchart.com/stocks/quotes/$CNDR/performance); [FXEmpire](https://www.fxempire.com/indices/cndr-ind_cbom)

**Defined-risk vs undefined-risk versions (most relevant given the user's constraint)**
- (Cboe-commissioned study, Jul 1986 to Dec 2015, about 29.5 years) Highest annualized returns: BXMD 10.66% and PUT 10.13%. Lowest annualized standard deviation: CNDR (iron condor) 7.23% and PUT 10.16%. Option-selling indexes generally had S&P-like returns with lower volatility and drawdowns. Months with losses worse than -6%: S&P 500 15, CNDR 10, BFLY (iron butterfly) 2. The source of strength was SPX options usually being richly priced. — [Cboe IR news release, Feb 2016](https://ir.cboe.com/news/news-details/2016/Study-Analyzes-Performance-of-CBOE-SP-500-SPX-Options-Selling-Indexes-02-23-2016/default.aspx); [Release PDF](https://s202.q4cdn.com/174824971/files/doc_news/2016/02/study-analyzes-performance-of-cboe-spx-options-selling-indexes-pdf1691637391968.pdf); [Wilshire 2019 benchmark study](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/spx/wilshire-options-based-benchmark-indexes-2019.pdf)
- (EX, Cboe 2021) Over about 35 years, worst peak-to-trough monthly drawdowns were 19% for CNDR and 47.1% for BFLY, vs 51% for the S&P 500. The BFLY figure looks inconsistent with the "2 bad months" stat above and no methodology is given, so verify it before use. — [Cboe BFLY/CNDR insight](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/benchmark-indices-series-volatility-management-with-cboes-bfly-and-cndr-indices/); [BFLY methodology](https://cdn.cboe.com/api/global/us_indices/governance/BFLY_Methodology.pdf)
- (EX) Cboe SMILE fact sheet, Jun 1986 to Sep 2016: SMILE had a higher mean return than RXM, CNDR, and BFLY, with greater Sharpe and Sortino. CNDR and BFLY Sharpe values are not stated. — [Cboe SMILE fact sheet](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/indices/documents/cboe-smile-index-fact-sheet.pdf)
- **Cost of the wing:** the long OTM put that turns a short put into a spread is itself an overpriced put. AQR's tail-hedging review (about 35 years of index option data) finds OTM index puts gave negative returns in each decade because prices embed risk premia (implied vol and skew exceed realized outcomes). — [AQR Journal of Systematic Investing, Tail Risk Hedging](https://www.Aqr.Com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/Journal-of-Systematic-Investing-Vol-1-Issue-1--Tail-Risk-Hedging-AQR.pdf?sc_lang=en)
- (PR, J. Alternative Investments, Winter 2019) Israelov, "Pathetic Protection". Using the Cboe 5% Put Protection Index plus Monte Carlo, the worst 1% drawdowns of a put-protected portfolio were deeper than those of a "divested" portfolio (less equity) with the same compound excess return: 20-day about -9.6% vs -6.6%, and 250-day about -32.1% vs -20.9%. Divesting won in 97-100% of cases, especially when options carry a VRP. Implication for a small account: *sizing down is a cheaper drawdown control than buying wings*, but wings remain necessary to cap gap risk under margin constraints. — [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/pathetic-protection/); [Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2019/08/22/are-puts-efficient-at-protecting-downside-risk)
- (PRAC) A put spread can have a better *return on capital* than a naked put with well-chosen strikes, but a lower absolute return. The naked put ties up far more capital and carries crash risk (e.g., March 2020). — [DataDrivenOptions, credit put spread](https://datadrivenoptions.com/strategies-for-option-trading/favorite-strategies/credit-put-spread/)
- (PRAC, promoter, unaudited) WealthyOption backtested both and moved *from* spreads *to* naked SPX puts, recommending spreads for accounts that cannot run uncovered positions. Self-reported 2016-2026 max DD was 19% vs about 35% for SPX. — [WealthyOption](https://wealthyoption.com/)
- (PRAC) Credit spreads have negatively skewed payoffs. Example: collect $1 on a 10-wide spread, risk $9, so one max loss erases about 9 winners. Wider spreads with larger credits slightly improve expectancy but lower the win rate. — [fattail.ai](https://fattail.ai/credit-spread-options/)
- (PRAC) Call-side skew makes index call strikes closer than put strikes in delta and premium terms (a less favorable risk/reward for the call wing of an iron condor). One commenter reports index call credit spread backtests showing negative returns (anecdotal). — [Option Alpha SPY put credit spread backtest page and comments](https://optionalpha.com/blog/spy-put-credit-spread-backtest)
- (PRAC, Option Alpha, about 5-year SPY put credit spread backtest) Allocation matters as much as structure. At 10% of capital per trade: 39% return on capital, Sharpe 0.62. At 5%: 16% return on capital, Sharpe 0.56, but slightly higher return on risk (280% vs 272%). — [Option Alpha](https://optionalpha.com/blog/spy-put-credit-spread-backtest)
- No rigorous matched-delta head-to-head of iron condors vs strangles (Sharpe, max DD) was found. — (search gap; see Gaps)

### Inferences
- **Regime needed:** IV above expected realized vol (positive IV-RV spread) and contango in VIX term structure. Range-bound or slowly rising markets are best. Steep put skew favors put credit spreads and put-writing over strangles. The strategy suffers in (a) vol expansions with gap moves and (b) strong rallies, where the capped upside lags the index (2020 PUT +2.1% vs SPX +18.4%).
- **Typical retail rule set (practitioner consensus, not peer-reviewed):** enter 30-60 DTE (45 is the norm). Short strikes at 16-30 delta (strangle/condor 16-20 delta, put spread 20-30 delta). Take profit at 50% of credit (25% for strangles in some practice). Exit or roll at 21 DTE. Stops at 2-3x credit, or a defined-risk width. Optionally enter only when IVR is 30-50 or higher. The 50% exit raises win rate and lowers variance but also lowers total profit, and it raises cost drag (spintwig commissions finding).
- **Capital/margin:** cash-secured SPX/XSP puts need the full notional (SPX about $100 x index, XSP 1/10 of that). Defined-risk spreads/condors need width minus credit. Naked strangles need Reg-T or portfolio margin and higher option approval. Short-vol margin requirements jump in a vol spike, which forces liquidation at the worst prices. This is the actual mechanism of most retail blow-ups.
- **Defined-risk implications (user constraint):** a put credit spread is a short put plus a long further-OTM put. The long wing has negative expected return (AQR, Bondarenko), so spreads harvest *less* VRP per unit of premium than naked puts. In exchange they cap gap and margin-expansion risk, which is what kills small accounts. CNDR (iron condor with 0.20/0.05 deltas) shows the lowest volatility of the Cboe family, but its 2025 performance was flat and its upside in rallies is capped. For a small defined-risk account, the evidence-supported structure ranking for VRP harvesting is: (1) SPX/XSP/SPY put credit spreads (put skew is where the premium is richest), (2) iron condors only when the call side collects meaningful credit (call-side premium is thin because of skew), (3) iron butterflies (BFLY: fewer severe losing months in the 1986-2015 study, but larger pin and gamma sensitivity near expiry). Wing width trades off the wing premium paid against tail protection. Narrow wings increase the number of contracts needed for a given credit, and with it commission and slippage drag.
- **How it blows up:** leverage (sizing on margin rather than notional), naked short calls/puts through gaps, stops that fill at spread-widened prices (the Aug 2024 VIX/put-spread widening is the canonical example), and short VIX ETPs (path-dependent daily rebalancing). The VRP may also be shrinking (Dew-Becker & Giglio), so backtests from 1990-2015 likely overstate forward returns.

## 2. Directional swing trades with defined risk (debit spreads, long calls/puts driven by trend/momentum/mean-reversion): do options help or hurt vs the underlying?

### Takeaway
Little direct academic evidence shows that expressing a stock-level signal through long options beats trading the stock. The general finding that options, especially OTM puts and short-dated contracts, are overpriced on average means long-premium directional trades start with a negative carry the signal must overcome. Momentum *within* option returns (straddle momentum) is strong pre-cost but roughly zero after paying full spreads.

### Cited Findings
- (PR, Journal of Finance 2023, vol 78, pp 3141-3192) Heston, Jones, Khorram, Li & Mo, "Option Momentum". Monthly ATM straddle returns on individual equities. Options with high past returns over 6-36 months keep outperforming. The pre-cost Sharpe is at least 3x that of standard stock momentum, and there is no long-run reversal. *Average option momentum returns are close to zero after paying the full bid-ask spread*, even restricting to below-median-spread options. Cost mitigation includes extreme deciles, excluding wide-spread options, and combining momentum with reversal signals. No crash risk was detected, though the authors warn the sample may be too short. — [Wiley JF](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.13279); [Working paper PDF](http://faculty.marshall.usc.edu/Christopher-Jones/pdf/opmom.pdf); [SSRN 4113680](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4113680)
- (WP) The same authors find seasonal (quarterly-lag) momentum in option returns. — [SSRN 4167231](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4167231)
- (PR/WP) Coval & Shumway: puts earn below the risk-free rate on average. Bondarenko: SPX puts are significantly overpriced. Both imply negative expected returns for long-put directional hedges and trades. — [arXiv 2608.24786 lit review](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.24786)
- (PR, Muravyev & Pearson) Equity option effective spreads are about 2.2% of option value on conventional measures. Traders who time executions pay less than 40% of the conventional measure. — [SSRN 2580548](https://www.ssrn.com/abstract=2580548); [CXO summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-options/timing-option-trades-to-suppress-trading-frictions/)

### Inferences
- A swing signal (for example, 20/50-day trend or RSI(2) mean reversion) with a modest edge on the stock, say +0.5-1% per trade, can be fully consumed by option spread (about 2% of premium per side for single-stock options) plus theta and VRP drag on long premium. Debit *spreads* partially offset this: the short leg sells overpriced IV and skew, and net vega and theta are smaller. They work best when IV is low (low IVR), and they cap upside.
- Options do add defined risk (no gap-through-stop losses), leverage without margin calls, and convexity for trend-following signals with fat right tails. The cost is a lower win rate and theta decay. Short-dated (under 2 weeks) long options decay fastest. Swing traders commonly use 30-60 DTE, 0.60-0.70 delta calls, or vertical spreads, to keep theta small relative to the expected move.
- Failure modes: correct direction but too slow (theta), IV crush after entry (buying after a spike), wide spreads on single stocks, and over-leverage through cheap OTM options (lottery-ticket behavior with negative expected value).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found that directly compares the same retail technical signal executed via stock vs debit spreads vs long calls with realistic costs post-2020. A targeted backtest would be needed.
- Option Alpha / ORATS directional-overlay research was not retrieved.

## 3. Term-structure trades: calendars and diagonals

### Takeaway
Cross-sectional academic evidence shows that the slope of the IV term structure predicts straddle returns. Options with an upward-sloping term structure (long-term IV above short-term IV) earn more, and an inverted term structure predicts poor returns for short-dated long options. That is a regime condition favoring short-front / long-back calendars when the front is rich (inverted). The evidence is mostly pre-2015 and gross of realistic costs.

### Cited Findings
- (WP/EFMA 2015; SSRN 1944298) Vasquez, "Equity Volatility Term Structures and the Cross-Section of Option Returns". Straddle portfolios with high slope (long-term minus short-term IV) outperform low-slope portfolios significantly. The result is not explained by standard factors, higher-order option factors, or jump risk. Slope regression coefficient 0.253 (Newey-West t=5.60) in one version and 0.201 (t=2.80) in another. A secondary summary cites gross 27.1%/month and net 12.3%/month for long-high/short-low, Jan 1996 to Jun 2007 (not from the paper itself). — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1944298); [EFMA 2015 PDF](https://efmaefm.org/0efmameetings/efma%20annual%20meetings/2015-Amsterdam/papers/EFMA2015_0530_fullpaper.pdf); [CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/volatility-effects/exploiting-the-implied-volatility-term-structure/)
- (WP) Jones & Wang (2012): IV slopes positively predict short-term ATM straddle returns (long-short about 7%/month). No clear relation exists for longer-term straddles. — [Jones & Wang PDF](https://msbfile03.usc.edu/digitalmeasures/christoj/intellcont/jones_wang-1.pdf)
- One study builds a calendar-spread long-short (decile 10 vs decile 1, 6-month vs 1-month options). Returns could not be verified from snippets. — [AUT/ACFR paper](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/264591/Informed_Option_Trading_Term_20190414.pdf)
- (PRAC) Alpha Architect summary of equity term structure and option returns. — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/equity-term-structure-and-option-returns/)

### Inferences
- **When calendars and diagonals work:** front-month IV elevated relative to back months (inverted term structure, often from an event in the front cycle) that then normalizes, and the underlying pins near the strike. They suffer on large moves away from the strike in either direction, on a parallel IV collapse (long vega in the back month), or when the event gets repriced into the back month. Index calendars in steep contango (normal state) offer little edge on term structure alone.
- Diagonals (long a deeper-ITM back-month option, short an OTM front-month option, the "poor man's covered call") give directional exposure with reduced capital. They share the covered-call failure mode: capped upside and full downside, less the premium.
- Multi-expiry, multi-leg structures increase bid-ask cost (see Section 7). Academic straddle-portfolio returns are not directly transferable to retail calendars.

### Gaps
- No post-2020 study of systematic calendar or diagonal returns on SPX/SPY or single stocks was found. No practitioner backtest (spintwig/ORATS) of calendars was retrieved.

## 4. Event plays: earnings IV crush, pre-earnings run-up, mispricing of earnings moves

### Takeaway
The peer-reviewed evidence is mixed and leans against the retail "always sell earnings IV crush" thesis. Gao, Xing & Zhang find that long straddles bought about 3 days before earnings and closed 1 day after earned about +3.3%, which means implied moves were *underpriced*. Later work finds negative straddle returns tied to retail overpayment or to jump-risk compensation (concave IV curves), and weekly-options evidence suggests pricing is roughly efficient. Costs dominate on smaller names.

### Cited Findings
- (PR) Gao, Xing & Zhang (2018), "Anticipating Uncertainty: Straddles Around Earnings Announcements". ATM straddles formed 3 days before announcements and closed 1 day after return a significant +3.34%. Investors underestimate earnings uncertainty, and low IV is associated with more positive straddle returns. — [Xing PDF](https://www.ruf.rice.edu/~yxing/straddle_201305_03.pdf)
- (PR, J. Empirical Finance) Straddles bought before earnings earn positive returns, unlike straddles generally. Traders underestimate volatility before and overestimate it after announcements, especially after high-vol periods. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0927539816300743)
- (PR/WP) De Silva, So & Smith (2023): retail investors attracted to earnings with anticipated volatility spikes overpay for ATM options, producing negative straddle returns. — [summarized in search; see Review of Finance paper](https://academic.oup.com/rof/article/29/4/963/8079062)
- (PR, Review of Finance 2025, vol 29(4)) "Pricing event risk: evidence from concave implied volatility curves". Negative average earnings straddle returns appear *only* when IV curves are concave, which captures jump risk. For large liquid firms a purely irrational explanation is less plausible. — [Review of Finance](https://academic.oup.com/rof/article/29/4/963/8079062)
- (PR) Chen, Gan & Vasquez (2023): earnings straddle returns are mostly driven by the jump component of prices. — [summarized via ROF search result](https://academic.oup.com/rof/article/29/4/963/8079062)
- (PR, J. Risk Financial Mgmt 2023) Weekly options around earnings: no association between straddle returns and historical vol, historical earnings vol, IV, or the IV-HV gap, so weekly prices may be efficient. — [MDPI JRFM](https://www.mdpi.com/1911-8074/16/5/270)
- (PRAC) BSIC "Straddling outside and into earnings" notes that transaction costs drastically reduce straddle performance, especially in smaller companies. — [BSIC](https://bsic.it/straddling-outside-and-into-earnings-part-ii-2/)
- (Vendor, unverified methodology) Selling straddles on "overpriced vol" earnings names: 63.4% win rate, 6.8% average return. — [iPresage](https://www.ipresage.com/research/earnings-iv-crush)

### Inferences
- **Pre-earnings run-up (long vol into the event):** the academic sign supports buying straddles a few days before and exiting *before or just after* the announcement, which captures the IV ramp and the underpriced move. Bid-ask cost on single stocks is the main hurdle.
- **Short IV crush (sell straddle/condor into the print):** high win rate with fat left tails (gap moves of 2-3x the implied move). The edge, if any, is conditional (concave IV curves, retail-heavy names, large caps). Use defined-risk iron condors or flies sized to tolerate a full max loss on each event. Earnings outcomes are not diversified within one name and cluster by sector.
- Mechanics: weekly expiries right after the event concentrate the IV crush. American-style single-stock options add early-assignment risk on short ITM legs post-event.

### Gaps
- Post-2020 replication of Gao-Xing-Zhang with realistic fills was not found. The data periods of the main papers (largely 1996-2010s) were not confirmed from primary text.
- No reliable source quantifies implied vs realized earnings-move ratios for 2021-2026.

## 5. Single stocks vs index/ETF options: liquidity, bid-ask, early assignment, pin risk, dividend risk

### Takeaway
For a retail systematic seller, SPX/XSP (European, cash-settled, Section 1256) remove early-assignment, dividend, and share-delivery risk and get 60/40 tax treatment. SPY/QQQ/IWM are liquid but American-style with dividend early-exercise risk on short calls. Single-stock options have wider relative spreads (about 2% effective), jump/earnings risk, and assignment/dividend/hard-to-borrow risks.

### Cited Findings
- SPX is European-style and cash-settled, with no delivery of shares and no early assignment. Dividends have no impact on SPX options. Under Section 1256, P&L is taxed 60% long-term / 40% short-term regardless of holding period. XSP is a mini-SPX at 1/10 size (similar notional to SPY). — [Cboe SPX options](https://www.cboe.com/tradable_products/sp_500/spx_options/); [Cboe XSP weeklys](https://www.cboe.com/tradable_products/sp_500/mini_spx_weekly_options/specifications/)
- Dividend risk on American calls: if a short call is ITM before ex-dividend and the dividend exceeds the remaining time value, the holder has an incentive to exercise early. The writer then delivers shares *and* owes the dividend. The only sure fix is closing the short call before ex-date. — [Cboe "Don't get stuck paying the dividend"](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/dont-get-stuck-paying-the-dividend-on-your-short-trade)
- Hard-to-borrow conditions can also trigger early call exercise. Per OCC, holders exercise only about 7% of options. Assignment is random. — [E*TRADE assignment risk](https://us.etrade.com/knowledge/library/options/understanding-assignment-risk); [Schwab](https://workplace.schwab.com/story/risks-options-assignment); [IBKR](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/trading-lessons/exercise-and-assignment/)
- Pin risk: an American-style option near the strike at expiry can be exercised after the close (even when OTM), leaving the writer unsure of the resulting share position. — [Cboe SPX page](https://certification.cboe.com/tradable_products/sp_500/spx_options); [IBKR](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/trading-lessons/exercise-and-assignment/)
- Equity option effective spreads are about 2.2% of option value (conventional), and timed executions pay under 40% of that. — [Muravyev & Pearson SSRN](https://www.ssrn.com/abstract=2580548)

### Inferences
- Short spreads on American-style SPY/stocks: a short leg assigned early (dividend or deep ITM put) can leave a small account holding 100 shares x the underlying, with a margin call over the weekend, while the long leg is still open. Close or roll short calls before ex-dividend dates and avoid holding short ITM legs into expiry.
- SPX notional (about $650k per contract at ~6,500) is too large for small accounts. XSP or SPY spreads are the practical substitutes. XSP is less liquid (wider spreads) than SPY.
- Single stocks add idiosyncratic jump risk (earnings, M&A, FDA). The VRP is less reliable there, and diversification needs many names, which multiplies costs.

### Gaps
- No quantitative comparison of current (2025-2026) quoted spreads SPX vs XSP vs SPY vs typical single stocks was retrieved.

## 6. Regime filters (VIX level, VIX term structure, IV rank/percentile, realized-vol, trend) and evidence they help

### Takeaway
Filters based on VIX term structure (exit or reduce short vol when the curve is in backwardation) have the best support: they cut risk with little cost to return in VIX-futures studies. IV level predicts short-vol option portfolio returns (Heston & Todorov). Evidence for IVR filters is practitioner-only and mixed: higher win rate and smaller worst loss, but fewer trades. No clean out-of-sample study of filters on an SPX put-write was found.

### Cited Findings
- (Academic study summarized by Quantpedia) Short VIX futures: moving to cash when the futures curve slopes down (backwardation) significantly reduces risk with "little detectable cost to expected returns", earning 3.4% four-factor alpha/month, Sharpe 0.36. — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/exploiting-term-structure-of-vix-futures)
- (PRAC) A VIX/VIX3M-based switch between short and long vol had Sharpe about 0.6 and diversified during equity drawdowns. — [Quantseeker](https://www.quantseeker.com/p/timing-short-volatility-with-vix)
- (WP, arXiv 2026) VIX term-structure ratio used as a convexity/stress proxy: contango means short vol earns carry, backwardation signals stress. Used as a diagnostic in a backtest-evaluation paper. — [arXiv 2604.18821](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.18821)
- (PRAC) Since 2010, VIX backwardation occurred on 7.6% of trading days across 103 episodes. — [thetrading.tools](https://www.thetrading.tools/vix-term-structure). Specific thresholds (VIX/VIX3M < 0.95, M1/M2 contango > 10%) on some sites are uncited. — [Sharpnel](https://www.sharpnel-trading.com/learn/vix-term-structure/); [Volatility Box](https://volatilitybox.com/research/vix-contango-backwardation/)
- (WP) Implied variance predicts returns of variance-sensitive option portfolios across 20 futures markets. — [Heston & Todorov, AFA](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=41580)
- (PRAC) IVR of 50 or more raised the 45-DTE strangle win rate (93% vs 90% managed) and cut the largest loss ($721 vs $2,655 in low IV). — [wenxuecity repost of tastytrade](https://www.wenxuecity.com/blog/201507/60718/13280.html). A Nifty 45-DTE backtest (non-US) found that entering only in top-quartile vol raised the win rate from about 70% to about 86%. — [Zerodha Substack](https://inthemoneybyzerodha.substack.com/p/we-backtested-the-famous-45-dte-strategy)
- (PRAC) In Aug 2024, sellers who waited for VIX to retreat to the mid-20s fared better than those selling into the spike. — [TradeStation](https://www.tradestation.com/learn/options-education-center/navigating-market-chaos-options-strategies-for-volatility-spikes/)

### Inferences
- A practical filter stack for short premium: (1) VIX/VIX3M < 1 (contango), (2) IV-RV spread > 0 (for example, 30-day IV above 20-day realized), (3) IVR/IV percentile above some threshold, computed point-in-time, (4) optional trend filter (price above its 200-day moving average) for short puts. Filters mainly cut tail losses, and the Sharpe gain rests on a handful of episodes (2008, 2018, 2020, 2024-25). They are prone to overfitting, and backwardation exits often trigger *after* the gap.
- "High IV" entries carry higher premium but come during the highest-variance periods. Entering right after a spike (as VIX falls back into contango) is a common compromise.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed post-2020 study tests IVR, trend, or term-structure filters on an SPX put-write or iron condor with realistic costs.

## 7. Practical constraints: transaction costs and slippage on multi-leg orders, PDT rule (2025-2026 changes), options approval levels, small-account suitability

### Takeaway
The PDT $25,000 rule was eliminated. The SEC approved FINRA's Rule 4210 amendments on April 14, 2026, effective June 4, 2026 (FINRA RN 26-10), and replaced the rule with real-time intraday margin requirements. Broker implementation dates vary, and firms reportedly have until Oct 2027. For the user's under-$25k account, PDT no longer limits intraday exits or adjustments, provided the broker has implemented the change (check the broker's date). Intraday margin excess is now the binding constraint. Costs remain the main constraint: multi-leg spreads usually fill at a fraction of each leg's width, and frequent management (50%/21-DTE) can turn a thin edge negative.

### Cited Findings
- **PDT rule:** the SEC approved the change on April 14, 2026, removing the $25,000 minimum equity and the "pattern day trader" designation (in place since 2001). — [tastytrade](https://tastytrade.com/learn/markets/industry/pattern-day-trading/); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/options/articles/sec-eliminates-25-000-pattern-050417843.html). The replacement ties intraday buying power to real-time intraday margin excess rather than end-of-day day-trading limits. E*TRADE implemented the change June 9, 2026. — [E*TRADE](https://us.etrade.com/knowledge/library/margin/pattern-day-trading-rule-change). FINRA Regulatory Notice 26-10 (April 20, 2026) set the June 4, 2026 effective date, and the reported final compliance deadline for firms not yet ready is Oct 20, 2027. — [TradeZero](https://tradezero.com/en-us/blog/the-usd25-000-day-trading-minimum-is-gone-here-s-what-it-means-for-you). Search summaries reference SEC Release No. 34-105226. Reports differ on the freeze mechanics: one says repeated failures to meet intraday deficits within 5 business days trigger a 90-day freeze ([Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/options/articles/sec-eliminates-25-000-pattern-050417843.html)), and another describes it as a deficit-based mechanism, not the old PDT freeze ([Day Trading Toolkit](https://daytradingtoolkit.com/market-insights/pdt-rule-eliminated-2026-complete-guide)). Option Alpha mentions a $2,000 minimum (likely the standard margin-account minimum), which is unconfirmed against FINRA text. — [Option Alpha](https://optionalpha.com/blog/pdt-rule-change-what-it-means-for-options-traders). *Caveat: all PDT sources are broker or education sites. FINRA RN 26-10 and the SEC release were not fetched directly.*
- **Multi-leg slippage (vendor assumption):** ORATS models fills at 75% of bid-ask width for 1-leg, 66% for 2-leg, 56% for 3-leg, and 53% for 4-leg trades. It samples quotes about 14 minutes before the close rather than using closing prints. — [ORATS blog](https://orats.com/blog/help-my-backtest-isnt-working); [ORATS methodology](https://orats.com/university/backtesting-methodology)
- **Academic cost evidence:** conventional effective spreads are about 2.2% of option value for equity options. Timed executions pay under 40% of conventional measures. Practitioners use 40-60% of the quoted spread as a planning figure. — [Muravyev & Pearson, SSRN](https://www.ssrn.com/abstract=2580548); [CICF paper](https://www.cicfconf.org/sites/default/files/paper_745.pdf)
- **Index option impact:** price impact vs bid-ask in the index option market. — [ScienceDirect, J. Financial Markets 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1386418121000550)
- **Management cost drag:** spintwig's SPY 45-DTE wheel with 50%/21-DTE management became unprofitable after commissions at 10-delta and higher. — [spintwig](https://spintwig.com/spy-wheel-45-dte-options-backtest)
- **Approval levels:** brokers tier option approval. Level 3/4 covers spreads and naked writing, with corresponding assignment risks. — [E*TRADE Level 3/4 assignment risk](https://us.etrade.com/knowledge/library/options/understanding-assignment-risk)

### Inferences
- With PDT gone, small accounts can now run intraday management (stops, same-day adjustments) without the 3-day-trade limit. Swing strategies were only mildly affected anyway, since holds of days to weeks are not day trades. Intraday margin calls are now the binding constraint, and a vol spike can trigger intraday margin deficits.
- **Small-account (under $25k), defined-risk sizing (inference built from the cited evidence):**
  - Size on *max loss* (width x 100 minus credit), not on buying power or credit. Per-position max loss of 1-3% of equity (for example, $200-$750 on $25k). For a 5-wide SPY/XSP put spread collecting $1.00-$1.50, max loss is $350-$400 per spread, so about 1-2 contracts each.
  - Cap aggregate correlated short-vol max loss (all SPX/SPY/QQQ/IWM short-put-side exposure together) at about 10-20% of equity. In Aug 2024 and April 2025, every index short-put position moved against the trader at once. The Option Alpha allocation test (5% vs 10% per trade) shows higher allocation raises return on capital but not return on risk.
  - Ladder entries across expiries and weeks (e.g., 1-2 new positions per week at 35-50 DTE) so that one entry date does not dominate. This mimics the reduced path-dependence of WPUT (weekly) vs PUT (monthly): WPUT max DD -24% vs -33% (1986-2015, CXO).
  - Prefer cash-settled European XSP for no early assignment and Section 1256 tax treatment, if spreads are acceptable. Otherwise use SPY/QQQ/IWM and manage ex-dividend and ITM short legs before expiry.
  - Israelov's result implies that holding cash (smaller size) is a more efficient drawdown control than buying extra protective wings. Defined-risk wings are for capping single-trade gap loss, not for making larger size safe.
- Small-account suitability (inference): under about $10k, use defined-risk spreads on SPY/XSP/IWM/QQQ with 1-5 point widths. Risk 1-3% of equity per position (max loss) and cap aggregate short-vol risk so that a simultaneous max loss on every open position stays survivable. Avoid naked strangles; they need margin approval and are unlimited-risk. Commissions of about $0.50-0.65 per contract per leg matter a lot on narrow spreads collecting $0.30-1.00.
- Enter multi-leg orders as a single combo order at or near mid, and walk the price. Do not leg in an automated system unless legging risk is modeled.

### Gaps
- Primary FINRA RN 26-10 and SEC release text were not retrieved. Exact new intraday margin rules (minimum equity, freeze terms) need verification against FINRA or the broker.
- Broker-specific option-approval criteria were not compiled.

## 8. Backtesting pitfalls specific to options

### Takeaway
The main ways an options backtest overstates results are mid-price or closing-print fills, ignoring per-leg spread and commission, parameter mining over DTE and delta, backfilled index histories, look-ahead in IV rank or percentiles, ignoring early assignment and dividend effects on American options, and ignoring margin expansion that forces liquidation. A realistic model charges a fraction of each leg's spread, calibrated to liquidity and leg count, and stress-tests that fraction.

### Cited Findings
- Mid-price fills are optimistic, and charging 100% of the spread is conservative. Model fills as a fraction of each leg's width. A short straddle that looks steadily profitable at mid can flatten or turn negative with bid/ask fills and fees. — [ORATS](https://orats.com/blog/help-my-backtest-isnt-working); [OptionsTradingIQ slippage](https://optionstradingiq.com/options-slippage/); [Zudora](https://zudora.in/articles/how-to-backtest-an-options-strategy)
- Closing prints can be stale or illiquid, so ORATS uses quotes about 14 minutes before the close. ORATS warns against testing 10 DTEs and picking the best, and suggests checking that neighboring parameters also work. — [ORATS methodology](https://orats.com/university/backtesting-methodology); [ORATS blog](https://orats.com/blog/help-my-backtest-isnt-working)
- Backfilled history: much of PUT (pre-2007) and WPUT (pre-2015) history is retroactively constructed. — [CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-options/performance-of-cboe-putwrite-indexes/)
- Effective vs quoted spreads: conventional midpoint-based measures are biased, and execution timing changes realized cost. — [Muravyev & Pearson](https://www.ssrn.com/abstract=2580548)
- Option momentum alpha vanishes after full spreads, a cautionary example for any option cross-sectional signal. — [Heston et al., JF 2023](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.13279)
- VIX spikes are partly a quote-mechanics artifact (spread widening), so backtests that mark positions to VIX or to quoted mids during stress misstate the realized exit prices of stops. — [BIS Bulletin 95](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull95.pdf); [SEC DERA](https://www.sec.gov/files/dera-vix-working-paper-2504.pdf)
- Early assignment and dividend risk on American options (ignored in many backtests). — [Cboe dividend risk](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/dont-get-stuck-paying-the-dividend-on-your-short-trade)
- Drawdown denominators: spintwig drawdowns over 100% (for example, -314.58%) show that results reported on margin or buying power differ from notional. Compare like with like. — [spintwig](https://spintwig.com/spy-wheel-45-dte-options-backtest)

### Inferences
- **Look-ahead in IV rank:** IVR/IVP must be computed only from data available at the decision time (a trailing 252-day window, excluding the current bar if entry is at the open). Using full-sample min/max or end-of-day IV for an intraday entry is a common leak. Vendor "IV rank" histories may be recomputed with revised methodologies.
- **Survivorship:** single-stock option universes must include delisted or merged names and their final option payoffs. Restricting to today's liquid optionable names inflates short-put results, since bankruptcies are excluded.
- **Other items to model:** contract multiplier changes after corporate actions (adjusted options), weekly expiries not existing earlier in the sample (SPX weeklys and daily expiries arrived over the 2010s-2022), margin-requirement changes in stress (forced liquidation), regime dependence (a 2010-2019 low-vol bull sample overstates short-vol results), and the declining VRP (Dew-Becker & Giglio).

### Gaps
- No academic paper specifically quantifying IV-rank look-ahead bias or option survivorship bias magnitudes was found.

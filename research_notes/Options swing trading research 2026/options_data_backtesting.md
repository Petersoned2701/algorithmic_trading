## Data vendors: coverage, granularity, greeks, delivery, price, licensing

### Takeaway
For a days-to-weeks swing system, end-of-day or near-close chains with NBBO bid/ask (plus greeks/IV) are enough, and good ones are cheap. The best value retail options are **ThetaData** (from $40/mo, EOD on the free tier; API, NBBO-based EOD built at 17:15 ET; greeks need Standard at $80/mo), **ORATS Near-EOD** (2007 onward, 14-minutes-before-close snapshot with greeks and smoothed values; about $599 one-time for history or $99/mo recurring, per its page), and **Cboe DataShop Option EOD Summary** (2012 onward, 15:45 and close snapshots, $25-35 per symbol-month or $300-420/mo for the full market). **Massive (formerly Polygon.io)** is cheap ($29/mo Starter) for raw OPRA trades and aggregates but has no historical greeks. **Databento** is the high-fidelity tick/NBBO choice (OPRA Standard plan $199/mo at launch). **OptionMetrics IvyDB** is institutional/academic only, with no public pricing.

### Inferences
- For swing holding periods, an EOD or near-close NBBO snapshot plus greeks covers almost all needs. Tick or 1-min data (Databento, ThetaData Standard/Pro, ORATS 1-min) mainly matters for entry and exit timing studies and is overkill for an MVP.
- Cheapest credible paid path: ThetaData Value at $40/mo gets EOD and 1-min NBBO from 2020, but compute greeks yourself or pay $80 for Standard. Alternatively, a one-time ORATS Near-EOD purchase (~$599) gives 2007+ with delisted names and vendor greeks: one payment, no monthly lock-in.
- Massive is good for raw trades and aggregates, but its lack of historical greeks or IV and uncertain options-quote depth make it weaker than ThetaData or ORATS for chain-level option selection backtests.
- Broker APIs (IBKR, Schwab, Tradier) cannot supply expired contracts, so they are only useful for forward data capture and live trading, not backtests. Alpaca's history begins Feb 2024.

## Data quality issues: snapshot timing, bid/ask vs mid, stale quotes, missing strikes, adjustments

### Takeaway
Snapshot timing and fill assumptions affect results more than vendor choice. Close-time quotes widen sharply, so vendors use 15:45 (Cboe) or about 15:46 (ORATS) snapshots, while ThetaData uses the last NBBO at 17:15 report time. Mid-price fills overstate edge, and adjusted contracts (splits) and expired or delisted symbols must be handled explicitly.

### Cited Findings
- Cboe adds a 15:45 ET NBBO snapshot because it is "considered a more accurate representation of market liquidity compared to end-of-day". — [Cboe DataShop mirror](https://datashopcert.livevol.com/option-eod-summary)
- ORATS samples 14 minutes before close to avoid spread deterioration, and argues a 3:46pm ET quote is tighter and more representative than closing prints. — [ORATS Near-EOD](https://orats.com/near-eod-data); [ORATS backtesting methodology](https://orats.com/university/backtesting-methodology)
- ThetaData's EOD quote is the last OPRA NBBO at 17:15 ET, which is post-close, so it reflects closing quotes (often wider). ThetaData notes some exchanges don't publish EOD data when they should, which is why it builds its own report. — [ThetaData Bulk EOD](https://http-docs.thetadata.us/operations/get-bulk_hist-option-eod.html); [ThetaData OHLC & EOD](https://thetadata.net/docs/Articles/Data-And-Requests/OHLC-EOD.html)
- Kaggle/OptionsDX-derived free SPY data uses 4:00 PM close snapshots. — [Kaggle SPY 2010-2023](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/dudesurfin/spy-options-eod-volatility-surface-2010-2023)
- Closing prints "can be stale, illiquid, or simply not representative of tradeable prices". ORATS simulates fills from quotes and uses slippage of 75% of the bid-ask width for single legs, falling to 53% for 4-leg spreads. — [ORATS: Help, my backtest isn't working](https://orats.com/blog/help-my-backtest-isnt-working); [ORATS backtesting methodology](https://orats.com/university/backtesting-methodology)
- Midpoint fills assume zero trading cost, and the distortion compounds with turnover. Nasdaq advises that in wide markets, mid fills are not realistic. — [DEV: The fill model is where backtests quietly cheat](https://dev.to/tradevodata/the-fill-model-is-where-backtests-quietly-cheat-4mhe); [Nasdaq: 7 options backtesting pitfalls](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/avoid-these-7-options-backtesting-pitfalls-2019-07-01)
- Two-sided bid/ask is preferable to stale last-trade prices for thin contracts. — [historicaldata.net](https://historicaldata.net/options.html)
- OCC adjusts contracts on splits and other corporate actions: strike, count, multiplier, deliverable, and symbol can change, and odd-ratio or reverse splits create non-standard deliverables. The OCC info memo is authoritative. — [OIC: Splits Happen](https://www.optionseducation.org/news/splits-happen); [tastytrade: options after a split](https://tastytrade.com/learn/trading-products/options/stock-split/); [Adjusted options: reading an OCC memo](https://theoptionsguide.com/adjusted-options-and-occ-memos.aspx)
- LEAN/ThetaData serve raw (unadjusted) prices, and LEAN uses a Security Master for adjustments. — [QuantConnect: Theta Data](https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/lean-cli/datasets/theta-data)
- A QuantConnect forum thread discusses stock splits interacting with options positions and buying power. — [QC forum: stock splits and options positions](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/17754/stock-splits-and-options-positions-and-buying-power-requirements/)
- Suggested screening checks: crossed markets (bid > ask), zero bids, and quotes unchanged for many days, which indicate staleness. This is a search-summary recommendation, not a primary source.

### Inferences
- Pick one snapshot convention and use it consistently for signals and fills. Mixing a 15:45 signal with a 16:00 or 17:15 fill introduces look-ahead or spread bias.
- For a swing system, a reasonable default is to fill at the near-touch: buy at the ask and sell at the bid, or use ORATS-style partial-spread slippage. Report the mid-fill result only as an upper bound.
- Filter universes by open interest, volume, and relative spread at entry to avoid stale or missing-strike artifacts. Keep each contract's OCC symbol and deliverable rather than assuming a 100x multiplier.
- Greeks differ by vendor model (ThetaData uses Black-Scholes with a SOFR rate, ORATS uses smoothed SMV, Cboe uses its own Calcs). Recomputing greeks in-house from NBBO and the underlying gives consistency across vendors.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study was found that measures how much closing spreads widen versus 15:45 for US equity options. The claim rests on vendor statements.
- Missing-strike and coverage completeness stats per vendor were not found.
- How each vendor treats dividends and early-exercise in its greeks (European vs American model) was only found for ThetaData (Black-Scholes).

## Backtesting tools and frameworks for options

### Takeaway
**QuantConnect/LEAN** is the most complete option-aware engine: chains, OptionStrategies multi-leg orders, a strategy-aware margin model, a configurable early-assignment model, and free minute options data from 2012 in the cloud. Its weaknesses are cost and complexity, and its margin matching still has open bugs. **Option Omega** and the **ORATS backtester** are no-code SaaS for fast idea screening. **optopsy** (active, v2.3.0 Mar 2026) is the best lightweight Python library for chain-level strategy stats. **NautilusTrader** has new option-chain and greeks support but is oriented toward crypto venues. **backtrader** and **vectorbt** have no native options support. A custom pandas/polars EOD engine is often the pragmatic choice for swing horizons.

### Cited Findings

**QuantConnect / LEAN**
- Cloud includes US equity options (AlgoSeek, from 2012, minute) "freely harnessable" for backtests. The options feed is now free with any live trading server (announcement about 6 weeks before Oct 2026). — [QC US Equity Options](https://www.quantconnect.com/data/algoseek-us-equity-options); [QC equity and index options datafeed announcement](https://www.quantconnect.com/announcements/21283/equity-and-index-options-datafeed/)

Other 2026 reviews say $60/mo or $600/yr. The LEAN CLI requires a paid-tier organization. — [LuxAlgo QuantConnect review](https://www.luxalgo.com/blog/quantconnect-review-best-platform-for-algo-trading-2/); [newyorkcityservers QC review](https://newyorkcityservers.com/blog/quantconnect-review); [QC pricing](https://www.quantconnect.com/pricing)
- Multi-leg support: OptionStrategies objects can be bought or sold directly. OptionStrategyPositionGroupBuyingPowerModel margins recognized strategies (verticals, covered calls, collars, calendars, condors), and NullSecurityPositionGroupModel disables this. — [QC Buying Power docs](https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/writing-algorithms/reality-modeling/buying-power); [LEAN Option namespace](https://www.lean.io/docs/v2/lean-engine/class-reference/namespaceQuantConnect_1_1Securities_1_1Option.html)
- Known margin issues: #5693 (a spread can't be sized to the max margin). #9648 (strategy matching is non-deterministic across processes, so identical holdings can get different margin, sometimes double for a bear call spread). PR #9826 fixed unequal-wing iron condor margin to use the wider wing. — [Issue #5693](https://github.com/QuantConnect/Lean/issues/5693); [Issue #9648](https://github.com/QuantConnect/Lean/issues/9648); [PR #9826](https://github.com/QuantConnect/Lean/pull/9826)
- Assignment: DefaultOptionAssignmentModel scans hourly. It considers American options within 4 days of expiry and assigns short options that are at least 5% in the money when exercise is profitable after fees. It is configurable or can be disabled with NullOptionAssignmentModel. A third-party port claims it has no dividend-driven early-assignment rule (unconfirmed). — [QC Assignment docs](https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/writing-algorithms/reality-modeling/options-models/assignment); [DefaultOptionAssignmentModel reference](https://www.lean.io/docs/v2/lean-engine/class-reference/classQuantConnect_1_1Securities_1_1Option_1_1DefaultOptionAssignmentModel.html); [trade-engine PR #28](https://github.com/vinay-veerappa/trade-engine/pull/28)

### Inferences
- For a solo developer building a days-to-weeks options swing system, a reasonable split is a **custom pandas/polars (or DuckDB-on-Parquet) EOD engine** for research. Each day: select contracts by DTE and delta from the chain, enter at the ask or bid, mark at mid, exit on rules. optopsy can provide quick strategy-level priors. Use **LEAN** later for realism checks (assignment, margin, minute fills) and for live trading.
- SaaS backtesters (Option Omega, ORATS, OptionStack) are useful for quick hypothesis screening on index ETFs, but they cap tickers and history and don't export a reusable codebase.
- A custom engine must explicitly model: early assignment of short ITM calls before ex-dividend dates, expiration and pin handling, Reg-T vs portfolio margin per strategy, commissions per contract, and corporate-action adjusted contracts. None of these are free in pandas.
- NautilusTrader is promising but needs significant data-ingestion work for US equity options. It is not a quick start.

## Budget path: free/cheap to start, upgrade later

### Takeaway
A credible path is: **$0** (free SPY/QQQ/IWM datasets plus QuantConnect free cloud) → **~$40-80/mo** (ThetaData Value or Standard for EOD and 1-min NBBO plus greeks on any ticker, or a one-time ~$599 ORATS Near-EOD history) → **$200+/mo** only if intraday execution research or a live feed justifies it (Databento OPRA, ORATS API, Massive Advanced, Cboe full-market).

### Inferences (recommended low-cost starting stack)
1. **Phase 0 ($0, weeks 1-4):** Prototype a custom Python EOD engine (pandas or polars plus DuckDB over Parquet) on philippdubach SPY/QQQ/IWM 2008-2025 data. Cross-check signals in QuantConnect's free cloud tier, which has minute options data from 2012 and real assignment and margin models. Use optopsy for quick strategy priors.
2. **Phase 1 (~$40-80/mo):** Subscribe to ThetaData (Value at $40, or Standard at $80 for vendor greeks and tick) to extend to single-stock underlyings, recent data through today, and to snapshot your own daily chains. ThetaData also plugs into LEAN CLI locally (which needs a paid QC org). Alternative: buy ORATS Near-EOD 2007+ once (~$599) for a deep, survivorship-aware, pre-close snapshot dataset with greeks. Its ~15:46 timing matches a "decide near close, trade before close" swing workflow.
3. **Phase 2 (only if needed):** Use Databento OPRA ($199+/mo) or Cboe 15:45 Calcs for specific underlyings when fill-quality research or intraday entries matter. Use an OPRA live feed through the broker (Tradier, IBKR, Alpaca) for execution. Do not rely on brokers for history.
4. Start a forward archive early: save your own daily chain snapshots (e.g. from a ThetaData or Massive subscription) to Parquet so the dataset outlives any subscription. Licensing likely forbids redistribution, but personal archival use is the norm (vendor terms unverified).

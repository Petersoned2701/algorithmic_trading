# Harvest calm volatility, but budget for rate shocks

As of 7 October 2026 the US is in a **low-volatility but event-heavy regime driven by interest rates**. The Fed raised rates to 3.75–4.00% on 16 September. The 10-year Treasury sits near 5.3%, Brent crude near $100, and payrolls are weakening. Despite all that, the S&P 500 is at records and the VIX is about 15, in steep contango, with SPX implied volatility about 2.5–3 points above realized. Those conditions favor systematic, defined-risk premium selling. The core strategy the evidence supports is a laddered program of **30–45 DTE put credit spreads on SPY or XSP**, sized on maximum loss and gated by a VIX term-structure filter. Put-weighted iron condors, event calendars and bullish debit spreads are conditional add-ons that each still have to earn their place in a backtest. The premium itself is well documented. It is also shrinking, the protective wing of each spread gives some of it back, and every failure mode in the record comes from gaps, leverage, or fills that widen under stress. ## A hiking Fed and 5.3% yields sit beneath a 15 VIX

The macro backdrop is stagflation-tinged and two-sided. On 16 September the FOMC raised the target range 25bp to **3.75–4.00%**, its first hike since July 2023, by a unanimous vote ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/16/fed-rate-decision-september-2026.html)). The data have since cooled. September payrolls rose only **+29k** against a consensus near +84k. Unemployment ticked up to **4.2%**, and wage growth slowed to 3.0% y/y, the lowest since May 2021 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/02/jobs-report-september-2026.html)). After that report, Kalshi-based pricing gave roughly an **85% chance of a hold** at the 27–28 October meeting ([Forex Factory](https://www.forexfactory.com/news/1429690-the-us-employment-situation-september-2026)). Inflation is high in headline terms because of energy: August CPI was 3.4% y/y with core at 2.4% ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/11/cpi-inflation-report-august-2026.html)), core PCE was 3.0% ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/u-core-pce-inflation-rises-132052964.html)), and Brent is back above $100 as Iranian tanker attacks around Hormuz pick up again ([CNBC](https://cnbc.com/2026/10/06/crude-oil-tanker-strait-hormuz-iran-attack.html)). Fewer signals before a meeting tend to mean bigger surprises at it.

Rates are where the stress shows. The Fed's H.15 release had the **2-year at 4.84% and the 10-year at 5.31%** on 5 October ([Fed H.15](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h15/)), with the 10-year reported at its highest level since 2002 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/US2Y)). The 2s10s spread has bear-steepened to about **+47bp** ([FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/T10Y2Y)). Equities have ignored all of it. The S&P 500 closed at a record **7,818.93 on 6 October, +14.2% YTD** ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/stock-market-today-oct-6-134418919.html)), and the Nasdaq is up 18.7% ([WTOP/AP](https://wtop.com/national/2026/10/how-major-us-stock-indexes-fared-tuesday-10-6-2026)). Equity volatility is calm and priced rich. On 6 October spot VIX was **15.01 against VIX3M at 17.64, a ratio of about 0.85**, and the October VX future settled near 17.46 ([thetrading.tools](https://www.thetrading.tools/vix-term-structure)). SPX implied vol of about **13% sat above 30-day realized of about 10.4%** ([Unusual Whales](https://unusualwhales.com/stock/SPX/volatility)). During that spike SPX realized skew inverted to its most extreme level in over 20 years, as traders bid the right tail ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/spx-realized-skew-inverts-as-traders-focus-on-right-tail/)). The warning sign today is the bond-volatility (MOVE) index, which keeps making higher lows while the VIX makes lower highs. That divergence preceded equity turbulence in 2022, 2023 and at the start of the Iran war ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/daybook-us/2026/10/06/the-vix-of-bonds-is-rising-but-bitcoin-and-stocks-aren-t-hearing-it-yet)).

## Index put credit spreads are the evidence-backed core

The volatility risk premium (VRP) is one of the best-documented anomalies in options. Carr and Wu measured persistently negative variance risk premia in index options ([RFS 2009](https://engineering.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/2019-01/CarrReviewofFinStudiesMarch2009-a.pdf)). Coval and Shumway, and later Bondarenko, showed that SPX puts earn below the risk-free rate and are systematically overpriced ([literature review, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.24786)). Heston and Todorov find that the level of implied variance predicts returns on variance-sensitive option portfolios but not on the underlying ([AFA](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=41580)). That makes IV a timing variable for short-premium P&L, not a directional signal. A Cboe-commissioned study of 1986–2015 found the option-selling indexes delivered roughly S&P-like returns with less risk. The cash-secured PUT index returned **10.13% a year**. The CNDR iron-condor index had the lowest volatility at **7.23%**. Months worse than −6% numbered 15 for the S&P 500, 10 for CNDR and just **2 for the BFLY iron butterfly** ([Cboe](https://ir.cboe.com/news/news-details/2016/Study-Analyzes-Performance-of-CBOE-SP-500-SPX-Options-Selling-Indexes-02-23-2016/default.aspx)).

The recent record is weaker, and an honest forecast has to start there. From January 2007 to September 2025, PUT returned **6.7% a year with a 0.49 Sharpe and a −32.7% maximum drawdown**. The S&P 500 returned 10.7% with a 0.6 Sharpe over the same window ([Cboe PUT factsheet](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/indices/factsheet/CboeGlobalIndices_PUT-Index.pdf)). PUT also gained only 2.1% in 2020 while the index gained 18.4%. Dew-Becker and Giglio argue that the traded VRP has structurally declined ([SSRN 5525882](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/5525882.pdf?abstractid=5525882&mirid=1)), and pre-2007 PUT history is a retroactive reconstruction ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-options/performance-of-cboe-putwrite-indexes/)). The working assumption should therefore be a mid-single-digit edge with negative skew, not the double digits of the 1990s backtests.

The defined-risk constraint has a known price. A put credit spread is a short put plus a long, further-out-of-the-money put, and that long wing is itself an overpriced option. AQR finds OTM index puts lost money in every decade of roughly 35 years of data ([AQR](https://www.Aqr.Com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/Journal-of-Systematic-Investing-Vol-1-Issue-1--Tail-Risk-Hedging-AQR.pdf?sc_lang=en)). Israelov's "Pathetic Protection" goes further. Holding less equity beat put protection with the same expected excess return in 97–100% of simulated cases, and its worst-1% 250-day drawdowns were about −21% versus −32% for the put-protected portfolio ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/pathetic-protection/)). For this account, that means **small position size, not extra wings, should be the main drawdown control**. Wings exist to cap single-trade gap loss and margin expansion, which is what actually ends small accounts. The payoff is also lopsided: collecting $1 on a $10-wide spread risks $9, so one maximum loss erases about nine winners ([fattail.ai](https://fattail.ai/credit-spread-options/)). Puts remain the better side to sell, because put skew is where index premium is richest. Call wings normally collect less, and some practitioner backtests of index call credit spreads show losses ([Option Alpha](https://optionalpha.com/blog/spy-put-credit-spread-backtest)).

The standard practitioner rule set is reasonable as a starting point, but it is not peer-reviewed and has costs of its own. Enter at 30–45 DTE with the short strike at 20–30 delta, take profit at 50% of credit, and exit or roll at 21 DTE. In tastytrade's 45-DTE strangle study, closing at 50% of maximum profit lifted the win rate from 82% to 90% but cut total profit from about $6,535 to $3,710 ([tastytrade study repost](https://www.wenxuecity.com/blog/201507/60718/13280.html)). Frequent management also multiplies trading costs. spintwig found that commissions under 50%/21-DTE management made a 45-DTE SPY wheel unprofitable at 10-delta and above ([spintwig](https://spintwig.com/spy-wheel-45-dte-options-backtest)). The take-profit and DTE exits should therefore be swept as parameters, not adopted on faith. The regime filter with the best support is VIX term structure. In short-VIX-futures studies, moving to cash during backwardation cut risk "with little detectable cost to expected returns" ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/exploiting-term-structure-of-vix-futures)). Backwardation has occurred on only about 7.6% of trading days since 2010 ([thetrading.tools](https://www.thetrading.tools/vix-term-structure)), so the filter rarely keeps the system out of the market. IV-rank filters improve win rate and worst loss in practitioner data ([tastytrade study repost](https://www.wenxuecity.com/blog/201507/60718/13280.html)), but no peer-reviewed post-2020 study confirms them.

The failure record is consistent. In February 2018, short-VIX ETPs lost more than 90% in a day ([FAJ, Augustin et al.](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/0015198X.2021.1913040)), while unlevered PUT fell only about 4.8% that week ([Cboe](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/education/research_publications/after-the-volpocalypse-market-observation.pdf)). On 5 August 2024 the VIX printed near 66 pre-market while VIX futures stayed below 35 ([SEC DERA](https://www.sec.gov/files/dera-vix-working-paper-2504.pdf)). The BIS attributes much of that spike to dealers widening put quotes ([BIS Bulletin 95](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull95.pdf)), which is exactly the condition under which stop orders fill at terrible prices. In April 2025, a retail premium seller reported giving back a month of premium in the tariff shock ([Early Retirement Now](https://earlyretirementnow.com/2026/01/30/options-trading-series-part-14-year-2025-review/)). The losses come from leverage, correlated index exposure and stops that execute into widened spreads, not from the VRP being absent.

Translated into rules for a sub-$25k account (inferences built on the evidence above): size each position on **maximum loss (width × 100 minus credit) at 1–3% of equity**. A 5-wide SPY or XSP spread collecting $1.00–1.50 risks $350–400, so that means one or two contracts. Cap the combined maximum loss of all index short-put exposure at about 10–20% of equity, because SPY, QQQ and IWM spreads all lose together in a shock. Ladder one or two entries a week across expiries. The weekly put-write index's −24% maximum drawdown against −33% for the monthly PUT (1986–2015) shows how staggering entry dates reduces path dependence ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-options/performance-of-cboe-putwrite-indexes/)). Prefer **XSP** where its spreads are acceptable. It is European-style and cash-settled, so there is no early assignment, and gains get Section 1256 60/40 tax treatment ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/tradable_products/sp_500/spx_options/)). SPY's American-style short legs bring dividend-driven early-assignment risk ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/dont-get-stuck-paying-the-dividend-on-your-short-trade)). In today's regime, the IV–RV gap and contango justify running the program, but the low VIX means thin credits. Thin credits on narrow spreads magnify the drag from commissions and slippage, so the strategy needs to be tested at the credit levels actually on offer in October 2026.

## Condors, calendars and earnings trades must earn their place

The other defined-risk structures each fit the regime only partly. The supporting evidence for each is thinner or older than for put spreads.

| Structure | Fit with Oct 2026 regime | Evidence base | Dominant failure mode | Recommended role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SPY/XSP put credit spreads, 30–45 DTE | Strong: contango, IV > RV, uptrend | Academic VRP plus Cboe indexes; spread version weaker | Gap through strikes; correlated losses; stop fills in wide markets | Core sleeve |
| Put-weighted iron condors | Moderate: call side fights a record-high trend | CNDR study through 2015; 2025 roughly flat | Rally through the call wing; double the leg costs | Only when the call credit is meaningful |
| Iron butterflies | Moderate | Fewest bad months in the 1986–2015 study | Pin and gamma risk near expiry | Optional, low allocation |
| Calendars / diagonals | Event-specific: front-month event premium | Cross-sectional studies, pre-2015, before costs | Large move away from the strike; parallel IV collapse | Event sleeve, test first |
| Earnings structures | Season runs mid-Oct to mid-Nov | Mixed; signs conflict across papers | Costs; gaps 2–3× the implied move | Research only at first |
| Bullish debit spreads | Uptrend plus low IV makes them cheap | Weak; option-return momentum ≈ 0 after costs | Theta; correct direction but too slow | Prove the signal on stock first |

Iron condors add a call side that is usually underpaid because of skew. CNDR, which sells roughly 20-delta strangles with 5-delta wings, was about flat in 2025 ([Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/stocks/quotes/$CNDR/performance); [Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/benchmark-indices-series-volatility-management-with-cboes-bfly-and-cndr-indices/)). The 2026 right-tail skew could make call credits fatter than usual. One September snapshot showed SPX calls carrying more premium than puts ([Options Analysis Suite](https://www.optionsanalysissuite.com/index/spx/volatility)), but that source is weak. Meanwhile, the market is trending up at records on 29.5% earnings growth, and that is when short calls get run over. The defensible rule is to add the call side only when it brings in a meaningful share of the credit, and otherwise to run put-weighted, asymmetric condors. Cboe's BFLY figures conflict: two months worse than −6% in one study and a 47.1% maximum drawdown in another ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/benchmark-indices-series-volatility-management-with-cboes-bfly-and-cndr-indices/)). Butterflies should get only a small allocation until a backtest resolves the conflict.

Calendars rest on real but dated cross-sectional evidence. Options whose IV term structure slopes up outperform those where it slopes down ([Vasquez, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1944298)). A long-short version of that trade on short-term straddles earned about 7% a month in Jones and Wang ([Jones & Wang](https://msbfile03.usc.edu/digitalmeasures/christoj/intellcont/jones_wang-1.pdf)). Both results are before realistic costs and come from pre-2015 samples. In today's steady index contango, term structure alone offers little edge. The opportunity is event-specific: selling a front expiry that is inflated by CPI, the FOMC or the election, and buying the post-event month. An unverified, six-week-old market comment notes the November VIX contract carries an election premium ([3PeaksTrading](https://x.com/3PeaksTrading/status/2092365803719188869)). Treat that as a hypothesis to backtest across past midterms and FOMC cycles, not as a trade.

Earnings evidence actually leans the other way from the popular "sell the IV crush" trade. Gao, Xing and Zhang found ATM straddles bought three days before earnings and sold one day after returned a significant **+3.34%**, meaning implied moves were too low ([Xing PDF](https://www.ruf.rice.edu/~yxing/straddle_201305_03.pdf)). Later work finds negative straddle returns only in specific cases: when IV curves are concave, which signals priced jump risk ([Review of Finance 2025](https://academic.oup.com/rof/article/29/4/963/8079062)), or when retail investors overpay. Weekly-option evidence suggests earnings pricing is roughly efficient ([JRFM](https://www.mdpi.com/1911-8074/16/5/270)). Equity option effective spreads of about **2.2% of option value** ([Muravyev & Pearson](https://www.ssrn.com/abstract=2580548)) consume most of these edges on smaller names. Earnings trades belong in the research queue until a cost-aware backtest of the current season says otherwise.

Directional debit spreads face the hardest test. Long options start with negative carry, because options on average are overpriced. Even the strong "option momentum" effect in straddle returns falls to roughly zero after paying the full bid-ask spread ([Journal of Finance 2023](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.13279)). A vertical spread offsets part of that drag, because the short leg sells rich IV. The current low-IV, uptrending tape is about as good a setting as debit spreads get. Still, the right sequence is to prove the trend or mean-reversion signal on the underlying stock first, then check whether its edge survives option spreads and theta. The macro notes favor a standing convex hedge, such as VIX call spreads, because of the MOVE divergence. The AQR and Israelov evidence says such hedges lose money on average. The defined-risk wings already cap per-trade loss, so the better response is smaller aggregate size plus the term-structure filter. A rate-volatility input (MOVE or a 10-year threshold) could be added as an experimental risk-off trigger, since that is where this regime's shock is most likely to come from.

Alpaca's free options feed is "indicative," a randomized derivative of OPRA that Alpaca staff say should not be used for live trading, so real quotes cost **$99/month, or $1,188 a year** ([Alpaca docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/v1.4.2/docs/about-market-data-api); [Alpaca forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/how-to-get-the-current-market-price-of-an-option-shown-in-alpaca-ui/14769)). Compare a realistic cadence of about 1.5 two-contract spreads a week, mostly closed early. That comes to roughly 12 contracts a week, or about **$400 a year at IBKR's $0.65**, plus a data bundle that IBKR waives above $30 a month in commissions ([IBKR KB](https://ibkb.interactivebrokers.com/es/tag/market-data?page=2)). The IBKR OPRA fee itself was not captured in the research. Alpaca remains a good choice for a developer who values a single REST API, static keys and automatic Level 3 in paper over paper realism. It also lacks SPX index-level data, so an XSP strategy would need a separate feed for the underlying. Tradier Pro is the cheapest live path at $10 a month with $0 equity-option commissions, but its delayed-data sandbox weakens the paper step. Whichever broker is chosen, the same broker should run both paper and live, so the order-construction code is never rewritten between phases.

## A one-time $599 dataset beats a monthly feed for swing backtests

Holding periods of days to weeks do not need tick data. A near-close NBBO snapshot of the full chain, with greeks and with expired and delisted contracts included, covers contract selection, entry, marking and exits. Broker APIs cannot supply that history. IBKR lists expired options as unavailable ([IBKR](https://interactivebrokers.github.io/tws-api/historical_limitations.html)), Tradier has no data for expired options ([Tradier](https://docs.tradier.com/docs/historical-data)), Schwab offers no option price history ([schwab-py](https://schwab-py.readthedocs.io/en/latest/client.html)), and Alpaca's options history starts in February 2024 ([Alpaca](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/historical-option-data)). That rules out every broker for survivorship-free backtests.

ORATS fits this system best. Its history reaches back through 2008, 2018, 2020 and 2022, the stress episodes that matter most for a short-premium strategy, and it includes delisted tickers. Its snapshot, taken about 14 minutes before the close, matches a "decide near the close, trade before the close" workflow and avoids the closing-quote widening ([ORATS](https://orats.com/university/backtesting-methodology)) that Cboe also cites for its 15:45 snapshot ([Cboe DataShop](https://datashopcert.livevol.com/option-eod-summary)). Snapshot timing matters more than the choice of vendor: signals and fills must use the same timestamp, or the backtest builds in look-ahead or spread bias. For forward data, ThetaData's free EOD tier, or its $40 Value plan, can archive daily chains to Parquet so the dataset outlives any subscription. They conflict in places (ORATS API tiers, ThetaData history lengths, whether Databento's OPRA price changed in June 2026) and need checking at purchase.

For tooling, the pragmatic choice is a **custom pandas/polars engine on DuckDB-over-Parquet** for daily-resolution research. Use **optopsy** (v2.3.0, March 2026, now with calendars and tiered slippage, [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/optopsy/)) for fast strategy priors. Use **QuantConnect LEAN** for realism checks and live routing. LEAN offers multi-leg OptionStrategies, strategy-aware margin and a configurable assignment model ([QC docs](https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/writing-algorithms/reality-modeling/buying-power)). It costs about $60–84 a month for a Researcher setup ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/blog/quantconnect-review-best-platform-for-algo-trading-2/)) and has open margin-matching bugs, including non-deterministic spread margin ([issue #9648](https://github.com/QuantConnect/Lean/issues/9648)). Option Omega's $24.99 Lite tier is useful for quick screens of SPX, SPY, QQQ and IWM ideas ([Option Omega](https://optionomega.com/pricing/)). Skip backtrader, whose last release was in April 2023 and which has no native options chains ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/backtrader/)). vectorbt handles multi-leg options awkwardly ([coriva](https://coriva.eu.org/en/vectorbt-tutorial/)), and NautilusTrader requires building US option instruments by hand ([Nautilus docs](https://nautilustrader.io/docs/latest/concepts/options/)).

The engine has to avoid the known ways options backtests overstate results. Fill at a fraction of each leg's bid-ask width. ORATS uses **75% for a single leg, 66% for two legs, 56% for three and 53% for four** ([ORATS](https://orats.com/blog/help-my-backtest-isnt-working)). Report mid-price fills only as an upper bound. Compute IV rank from trailing, point-in-time data only. Keep delisted names. Model ex-dividend early assignment on American short calls. Weight post-2010 results more heavily, given the VRP decline. Stress the fill fraction for crisis days, when quotes widen far beyond normal ([BIS](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull95.pdf)). A sensible path is $0 for the first month on the free SPY/QQQ/IWM data and the QuantConnect cloud. Next comes the one-time ORATS purchase plus free or $40 ThetaData forward capture. Spend $200+ a month on Databento, Cboe calcs or ORATS APIs only if intraday execution research justifies it.

## Conclusion

The binding constraint on this project is cost relative to account size, not strategy selection. At PUT's post-2007 return of 6.7%, a $15,000 account would expect roughly $1,000 a year *before* the defined-risk wing gives back part of the premium. A stack of Alpaca real-time data ($1,188), a QuantConnect seat (~$720–1,000) and a monthly data feed would cost more than that. Recurring costs should therefore stay in the low hundreds of dollars a year until the account grows. That argues for one-time data purchases, per-trade rather than flat-fee broker pricing, and doing research on local hardware. The backtest's assumed fill fraction and the paper account's measured fill fraction become the project's go/no-go metric. If paper fills on two- and four-leg orders land worse than the ORATS-style 66% and 53% of width, the thin credits on offer at a 15 VIX will not cover the costs.

The regime also changes what the risk filter should watch. Every documented short-premium blow-up began as an equity-volatility event, and the standard VIX-backwardation exit is built for that. In 2026 the likeliest shock comes from rates and oil: a 10-year breaking higher, a hawkish surprise from a Warsh Fed that issues less guidance, or a Hormuz escalation. Equity volatility may not register these until the gap has already happened. A system built now should test a rate-volatility trigger alongside the VIX term-structure filter. It should cut aggregate size, rather than skip trades, when a single expiry cycle spans CPI, the FOMC and the election. And it should not count on the 1990s-sized premium found in most published backtests.
